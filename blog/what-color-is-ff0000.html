What Color Is #FF0000? Understanding Hex Color Codes

Deep dive into the world's most famous hex code. Learn how hex colors work, the structure of a hex code, and why red is FF0000.

By Yao Xiangqian · Nov 8, 2026 · 1600 words · 8 min read
Advertisement

The Quick Answer

#FF0000 is pure red. It's the brightest, most saturated red you can display in a standard hex color code — 255 red, 0 green, 0 blue.

You've seen it a million times. But have you ever stopped to wonder why it's "FF" and not something else? Or why it's the most famous hex code in the world?

Breaking Down #FF0000

Every 6-digit hex color code follows the format #RRGGBB — two characters for red, two for green, two for blue. Let's split #FF0000 apart:

ChannelHex ValueDecimal ValueMeaning
Red (RR)FF255Maximum red
Green (GG)000No green
Blue (BB)000No blue

FF in hexadecimal equals 255 in decimal. That's the maximum value a single byte can hold — which is why it's the "fullest" red possible in 24-bit color.

Why Hexadecimal? Why Not Decimal?

You might be wondering why we use hex at all. Why not just write rgb(255, 0, 0) and be done with it?

There are a few historical and practical reasons:

  • Compact: 6 characters vs "rgb(255, 0, 0)" — shorter is better when you're writing thousands of colors in CSS.
  • Binary-friendly: Each hex digit = 4 bits. Two digits = 8 bits = 1 byte. It's a perfect fit for how computers store color data.
  • Early web tradition: Hex colors were used in the first versions of HTML and CSS, and the tradition stuck.
  • Copy-paste friendly: It's a single token — no commas, no spaces, no function syntax.
💡 Conversion trick: To convert a hex pair to decimal in your head: first digit × 16 + second digit. For FF: 15 × 16 + 15 = 255. That's it.

The History of Red in Digital Design

Red has always been special in computing. Here's why:

The first color displays

Early computers didn't have color at all — they had monochrome displays, usually green or amber phosphor. When color arrived in the 1970s and 80s, it often came in the form of RGB monitors — and red was always the "first" channel.

HTML and the birth of the web

In the early web (HTML 3.2, 1997), you could use 16 named colors. Red was one of them. The hex code #FF0000 was everywhere — links, error messages, emphasis. It was the web's first "attention" color.

Red in UI design today

Red still means "stop," "error," "danger," and "delete" in almost every interface. It's the most emotionally intense color — which is why you should use it sparingly. Too much red causes stress and alarm fatigue.

What Makes #FF0000 Special

  • It's one of the 16 original named web colors (just red in CSS).
  • It has the shortest 3-digit hex shorthand: #F00 (double each digit → #FF0000).
  • It's the purest primary color in the RGB model — no mixing of other channels.
  • It's used in everything from stop signs to error messages to YouTube's logo.
  • Red is the first color babies can see (after black and white).

Colors That Go With #FF0000

Pure red is powerful and hard to pair. Here are 5 color schemes that work:

1. Classic Red + Black

Red on black. Think Ferrari, Coca-Cola, or a deck of cards. High contrast, bold, classic. But use sparingly — it can feel aggressive.

2. Red + Cream

Red with warm off-white (#FFF8E7). Softer than red on white, feels vintage and premium. Great for editorial and fashion.

3. Red + Navy

Red with deep navy blue (#0F2B5B). Nautical, patriotic, trustworthy. The classic Americana palette.

4. Red + Teal

Red with teal (#14B8A6). Modern and bold — complementary colors that pop without being cliché. Popular in startup branding.

5. Red + Gray Scale

Red on a neutral gray background. The safe choice for dashboards and apps. Red signals errors and alerts; everything else is gray.

⚠️ Accessibility warning: Pure red (#FF0000) on white has a contrast ratio of only 3.99:1 — below the WCAG AA standard of 4.5:1 for body text. Use a darker red like #CC0000 or #D32F2F for text.

Red Around the World

Red carries different cultural meanings — something to consider if you're designing for a global audience:

  • China & East Asia: Red is lucky, prosperous, and celebratory. It's the color of Chinese New Year and weddings.
  • Western cultures: Red means danger, stop, love, and passion. It's also associated with communism and revolution.
  • India: Red represents purity, marriage, and fertility. Brides traditionally wear red.
  • South Africa: Red is associated with mourning.
