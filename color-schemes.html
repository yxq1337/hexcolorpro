Color Theory: The 6 Classic Schemes

🌈 Analogous

Colors next to each other on the color wheel. Creates a harmonious, serene feel. Think sunsets, ocean blues, forest greens. Great for backgrounds and ambient designs.

⚡ Complementary

Colors opposite each other on the wheel. High contrast, high energy. Think red-green, blue-orange, purple-yellow. Use sparingly for maximum impact — great for CTAs and highlights.

🔺 Triadic

Three colors evenly spaced around the wheel (120° apart). Vibrant and balanced. The classic "primary colors" scheme is triadic. Good for playful, youthful designs.

🎚️ Monochromatic

Variations of a single hue — different lightness and saturation. Super elegant and easy to get right. Perfect for minimalist designs, portfolios, and luxury brands.

🔀 Split Complementary

A base color plus the two colors adjacent to its complement. Less intense than complementary but still has good contrast. Versatile and visually interesting.

🔷 Tetradic (Double Complementary)

Four colors arranged as two complementary pairs. Rich with possibilities but trickier to balance. Best when one color is dominant and the rest support it.

💡 Pro Tips for Using Color Schemes

  • Follow the 60-30-10 rule: 60% dominant color, 30% secondary, 10% accent
  • Start with the mood you want — calm? energetic? luxurious? — then pick a scheme
  • Use neutral colors (white, gray, black) as your base, add color accents on top
  • Always check accessibility — use our Contrast Checker tool to verify readability
  • Less is more: start with 2-3 colors before adding more
